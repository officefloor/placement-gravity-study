# Kobori, Matsushita and Inoue (2015), Evolution analysis for accessibility excessiveness in Java, SANER 2015, 83–90

**Not obtained, 22 November 2026.** No open copy was found: OpenAlex lists no open-access
location; the IEEE copy is paywalled; the authors' laboratory pages did not yield the PDF; web
searches found only index records and two related Japanese-language reports by the same group (an
IPA report on extending ModiChecker, and an IPSJ technical report by Ishizue, Kobori, Matsushita
and Inoue on how often developers fix excessive modifiers), neither read.

What is known second-hand: the abstract (applying ModiChecker across versions of several OSS
projects; transitions of accessibility-excessiveness status; more change at major than minor
releases), and Vidal et al. (COMLAN 2016, §10): "they reported that the change of access
modifiers of methods is infrequent. They also found that the number of over-exposed methods and
fields tends to increase in time."

Bearing: if the second-hand report is right, excess exposure of fields and methods accumulates
over a system's life, per member, without being tied to the change that caused it. That is the
aggregate of H7's widening branch; the per-change attribution remains not found.
