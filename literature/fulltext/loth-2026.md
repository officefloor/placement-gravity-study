# Loth (2026), Coupling as a consequence of cohesion, Zenodo preprint (doi:10.5281/zenodo.21925200)

Unrefereed.

- Theory only. Within one "partitioning rule", coupling categories follow from cohesion (purity,
  completeness); at maximal cohesion all modularisation-controlled coupling is empty; the
  "coupling floor" is the necessary dependency graph; "every edge above it is a boundary defect or
  an accidental edge" (abstract).
- Corollary 2.2.2: "Since |R| is fixed for a given system, any increase in internalized
  dependencies forces an equal decrease in the boundary-crossing dependency count."
- "cohesion determines coupling's shape, not its size". No mention of encapsulation, access,
  private members, forces or class size. §7.3: the framework "does not supply the empirical bridge
  between formal compliance and real-world outcomes".

## Bearing

- Conservation: Corollary 2.2.2 is a conservation statement of a kind the plan's framing uses (a
  fixed total, divided by placement), but between internal and boundary-crossing *dependencies*,
  not between size and coupling. A reviewer could cite it; the plan's version adds size and an
  empirical test.
- H7: "every edge above the floor is a boundary defect" is the normative opposite of the plan's
  neutral stance (coupling is a cost paid for not concentrating). Not empirical.
