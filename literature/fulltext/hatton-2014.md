# Hatton (2014), Conservation of information: software's hidden clockwork?, IEEE TSE 40(5)

Read 22 November 2026 in its **preprint form only**: arXiv:1207.5027v1, "Power-laws and the
Conservation of Information in discrete token systems: Part 1 General Theory" (July 2012, 26 pp.),
in full. The TSE version was not obtained (paywalled); its text may differ.

- Proposes that "the Conservation of Hartley-Shannon Information plays the same over-arching role
  in discrete token based systems as the Conservation of Energy does in physical systems", and
  derives that this "implies power-law behaviour in component sizes in software systems no matter
  what they do or how they were built" (abstract). Data: about 90 packages, 55.5 MLOC in Ada, C,
  C++, Fortran, Java and Tcl.
- Nothing on coupling, placement, encapsulation or change.

## Bearing

- Conservation: as recorded on 20 November, the conserved quantity is token information and the
  prediction is the distribution of component sizes, not a trade between size and coupling. Stands.
- "no matter what they do or how they were built" is a claim of invariance under construction,
  i.e. of the kind "regardless of author". It is a precedent for the framing, at the level of a
  size distribution, not of placement.
